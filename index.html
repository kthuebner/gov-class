How to Play

Work through each section to build the strongest possible presidential candidate. You’re not designing someone good at being president — you want the person American voters will find most appealing.

This is a game of skill and luck. Choose the safe path or roll the dice on riskier traits. Just like in real life, becoming president has a lot to do with timing and being the right person for the moment.

Navigate using the tabs above. Complete all 7 sections, then submit your candidate in Section 8.

Section 4: Political Experience

"What previous political positions best prepare someone to become a winning candidate and successful president?"

Vice President
This is the highest level of experience below being president, but now you have to defend the record of the president you served with. You have experience, but it comes with baggage.
Luck plays a role. Doubling up changes the odds.
Governor
Being Governor is like being president of a state. Run a big state well and you look like a presidential hopeful. Run a small state poorly and you seem totally unqualified.
Luck plays a role. Doubling up changes the odds.
Senator
The role of Senator is pretty prestigious, especially if you were on important committees or getting major legislation passed. But if you were not, you might not seem ready for the job.
Luck plays a role. Doubling up guarantees the best outcome.
House Representative
The House is called the lower chamber for a reason. Unless you served for a long time and have serious accomplishments to your name, it is a pretty big leap to the presidency.
Luck plays a role. Doubling up guarantees the best outcome.
Secretary of State
In the 1800s the Secretary of State was seen as the President in Waiting. Six past presidents have had this job prior to becoming president.
No luck involved. Doubling up increases your score.
Cabinet Member
Working in the Executive Branch seems like it could be a good stepping stone to the White House. After all, the President is the Chief Executive.
No luck involved. Doubling up increases your score.
Total Outsider
You have very little political experience. You can run against the Washington establishment. The voters are frustrated with politics and politicians, but will they see you as totally unqualified or just the change we need?
High risk, high reward. Doubling up improves your odds.
