LeftandRight

This tool is an exploration of values, beliefs, and viewpoints, and how they connect to politics and policy. It won't tell you whether you are a Democrat or a Republican, liberal or conservative. Everyone holds a mix of beliefs and slightly different values, and as you click through you will probably agree with some viewpoints from both columns.

The goal is to give you a sense of the values behind the political spectrum and the ideas that shape different political coalitions. Keep in mind that these are based on generalizations and statistics. They aren't wild stereotypes, but they aren't perfect either.

Part 1 · Values and Beliefs

Read It Critically

Clear, memorable charts are also arguments. Before you accept this one, think about two things.

Generalization or stereotype?

A generalization describes a pattern, is backed by data, and allows exceptions: "Most Republicans favor the death penalty" (84%, Pew). A stereotype applies a fixed image to everyone: "Liberals oppose the death penalty," when about half of Democrats support it. Ask: Is it based on data? Does it allow exceptions? Would you assume it about someone you just met?

Averages hide the middle

Each party has real internal splits, and many people hold views from both columns. Libertarians, populists, and independents often land on both sides. Part 3 shows one way to see them.
