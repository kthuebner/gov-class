Two of these are special elections

Florida and Ohio are holding special elections to fill the remainder of terms that were vacated early, not regular six year contests. Both seats came open when their occupants left for the executive branch.

The Constitution gives states the power to fill Senate vacancies, and the Seventeenth Amendment lets governors appoint temporary replacements. Should an appointed senator be able to serve for years before facing voters?

Ten states redrew their maps

Districts are normally redrawn once every ten years, after the census. The next scheduled redraw is after 2030. Between the summer of 2025 and this year, ten states redrew anyway: Alabama, California, Florida, Louisiana, Missouri, North Carolina, Ohio, Tennessee, Texas, and Utah. Republicans expect to gain as many as 16 seats from the new maps. Democrats expect to gain as many as six.

Kansas tried. Republican leaders wanted a special session to redraw the 3rd District, and the state Senate had the two thirds needed to call one. The House fell short when ten Republicans would not sign the petition, so the Kansas map here is unchanged.

The states with dashed borders are drawn on their 2024 lines, because the new boundaries have not been published yet in a form this map can use.

Nothing in the Constitution says how often districts can be redrawn. Should there be a limit? Who should decide, and why would they be trusted to?

Kansas

- - - - -

This November Kansans elect a new governor, vote in a United States Senate race, and vote in their congressional district race. Further down the ballot are the state legislative races. All 125 seats in the Kansas House are up, because Kansas House terms run two years. The Kansas Senate is not. Those 40 seats were last elected in 2024 to four year terms and are next up in 2028.

Every one of you lives in Kansas's 3rd Congressional District. You do not all live in the same Kansas House district, and you do not all live in the same Kansas Senate district. Kansas has four congressional districts, forty state Senate districts, and one hundred twenty five state House districts covering the same ground, so the smaller the district, the more of them fit inside Johnson County.

Kansas did not redraw its congressional map. These are the same four districts you have had since 2022, and Blue Valley Academy sits in the 3rd.

The redraw that did not happen

In 2025 Republican leaders pushed to redraw the 3rd District, which would have split Johnson County. Redrawing required a special session, and calling one takes signatures from two thirds of each chamber.

Do the arithmetic. The Kansas Senate has 40 seats, two thirds is 27, and Republicans hold 31. They had the votes. The Kansas House has 125 seats, two thirds is 84, and Republicans hold 88. That is a margin of four. When ten Republicans declined to sign the petition, the number fell to 78 and the session was never held.

Ten signatures decided which district you live in. Should a map that shapes years of elections turn on a supermajority rule most voters have never heard of?
